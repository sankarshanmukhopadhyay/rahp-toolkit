# Kubernetes governance — bounded research snapshot

> This is a deliberately bounded research snapshot derived from the Apache-2.0 licensed Kubernetes Community `governance.md` at the pinned source ref above. It is modified by selection for RAHP research and is not presented as the complete or authoritative upstream document.

## Principles

The Kubernetes community adheres to the following principles:

- Open: Kubernetes is open source.
- Welcoming and respectful: community participation follows the Code of Conduct.
- Transparent and accessible: work and collaboration should be done in public.
- Merit: ideas and contributions are accepted according to technical merit and alignment with project objectives, scope, and design principles.

## Community groups

The project is comprised of Special Interest Groups (SIGs), Working Groups (WGs), and Committees.

### SIGs

The Kubernetes project is organized primarily into Special Interest Groups. The goal is to enable a distributed decision structure and code ownership while providing focused forums for getting work done, making decisions, and onboarding contributors.

Every identifiable subpart of the project is intended to be owned by some SIG.

SIGs must have at least one and ideally two chairs. Chairs are organizers and facilitators responsible for operation of the SIG and for communication and coordination with other SIGs, the Steering Committee, and the broader community.

Each SIG must have a charter that specifies its scope, responsibilities, areas of authority, how members and leadership roles are selected or granted, how decisions are made, and how conflicts are resolved.

SIGs should be relatively free to customize how they operate within broad guidelines and constraints imposed by cross-SIG processes and shared project assets.

Much work in a SIG should stay local within that SIG. However, SIGs must communicate in the open, ensure that other SIGs and community members can find notes of meetings, discussions, designs, and decisions, and periodically communicate a high-level summary of their work to the community.

### Committees

Some topics, such as Security or Code of Conduct, require discretion. Unlike SIGs, Committees do not have open membership and do not always operate in the open.

The Steering Committee can form committees as needed for bounded or unbounded duration. Membership of a committee is decided by the Steering Committee. A committee has a charter and a chair and will report to the Steering Committee periodically and to the community as appropriate to its charter.

## Cross-project communication and coordination

When efforts cross SIG boundaries, the SIGs are expected to coordinate with each other and come to mutually agreed solutions. Cross-SIG coordination intentionally carries additional overhead.

When a SIG needs to make changes or impose processes or conventions with project-wide impact, project-wide communication processes need to be followed. Proposals with project-wide impact need to be announced broadly, with an opportunity for other SIGs and community members to provide feedback and guidance.

The SIG that owns the area according to its charter owns the decision. In cases of extended debate or deadlock, decisions may be escalated to the Steering Committee.

## Contributor obligation

All contributors must sign the CNCF contributor agreement as described by the project.
