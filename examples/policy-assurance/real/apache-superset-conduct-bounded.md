# Apache Superset Code of Conduct — bounded research snapshot

> This is a deliberately bounded research snapshot derived from the Apache-2.0 licensed Apache Superset `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` at the pinned source ref above. It is modified by selection for RAHP research and is not presented as the complete or authoritative upstream document.

## Applicability

This code of conduct applies to spaces managed by the Apache Software Foundation, including public and private mailing lists, issue trackers, wikis, blogs, and other community communication channels.

The code is expected to be honored by people who participate in the Apache community formally or informally, claim affiliation with the Foundation, or represent the ASF in Foundation-related activities.

The code is not exhaustive or complete and is intended to be followed in spirit as well as in the letter.

## Specific guidelines

Participants should be open, empathetic, welcoming, friendly, patient, collaborative, inquisitive, professional, and careful in the words they choose.

Harassment and other exclusionary behavior are not acceptable. This includes violent threats, discriminatory language, posting sexually explicit or violent material, posting or threatening to post other people's personally identifying information, sharing private content without permission, personal insults, unwelcome sexual attention, repeated harassment, or encouraging such behavior.

If someone asks another participant to stop repeated harassment, the participant should stop.

Participants should keep communication concise where practical, stay on topic, and avoid repeating arguments already present in a discussion.

When a participant leaves or disengages from a project, they should communicate that they are leaving and take reasonable steps to enable others to continue the work. They should remain respectful and should not misrepresent the project's goals or achievements.

## Reporting guidelines

When a participant appears unaware of a guideline or violates the code, another participant may point out the code in public or private as appropriate. Any such message should itself remain respectful and non-abusive.

If someone believes another participant is violating the code, they may point out the code and should assume good faith where appropriate.

If difficulties remain, compliance concerns may be reported in confidence through the Foundation's identified reporting contacts or through the project-specific private reporting channel.

If a violation is present in documentation or code, participants are asked to report it privately to the project and, where they have authority, to resolve or remove the concerning material while remaining mindful of the reporting person's perspective.

## Slack community expectations

Participants should treat everyone in the community with respect. Harassment is not acceptable and will not be tolerated.

Participants should use the appropriate communication channel, ask thoughtful questions, avoid duplicate posting, communicate openly where practical, and avoid unsolicited direct messages for product or service pitches.

Participants should use discussion threads and lightweight reactions in ways that respect other participants' attention and improve information density.
